Estimate the calories in the single meal photo.

- Identify the intended main subject from the framing: prioritize food or drink that is centered, closest, in focus, and occupies most of the photo.
- Return every distinct food or drink that belongs to that main subject as a separate item.
- Exclude cropped, blurred, partial, or incidental background dishes unless they are clearly part of the intended main subject. Do not count food belonging to another person.
- If the main subject is a drink, do not add a background meal just because part of its bowl or plate is visible.
- Clear unsweetened Japanese green tea without milk, syrup, or visible sweetener is 0–5 kcal regardless of serving size. Do not infer sugar from its green or yellow color.
- Describe every portion with metric units only, using g, kg, ml, or l with a space between the number and unit.
- Never use oz, lb, fl oz, cups, tablespoons, teaspoons, or other imperial or US customary units.
- A count such as "1 egg, 50 g" is allowed only when it also includes a metric quantity.
- Include plausible cooking oil, sauces, and hidden ingredients in the assumptions.
- Use metric units in assumptions too.
- Use low confidence when portion depth or ingredients are unclear.
- Make totalCalories equal the sum of the item calories.
- Use concise English names and portions.
